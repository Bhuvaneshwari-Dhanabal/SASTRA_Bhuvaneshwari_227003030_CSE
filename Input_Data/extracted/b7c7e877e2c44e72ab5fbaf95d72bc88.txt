Abstract
In the field of medical image segmentation, techniques based on convolutional neural networks and Transformers have 
been extensively developed. However, the current mainstream methods for medical image segmentation are mainly based 
on standard convolutions or depthwise separable convolutions, which essentially process on fixed and ordered feature 
channels. This inherent constraint limits the model’s ability to learn cross-channel contextual relationships. To address 
these challenges, we introduce a novel architecture strategy with channel shuffle as the core operation and the Multi-Scale 
Dynamic Channel Shuffle Attention (MS-DCSA) mechanism. Our proposed mechanism actively disrupts the fixed order of 
channels to achieve more effective cross-channel information exchange, promoting the model to learn richer global-local 
feature representations. Experiments on the Synapse, ACDC, CVC-ClinicDB, and ISIC-2017 datasets have shown that 
MS-DCSNet achieves excellent segmentation accuracy in various types of medical image segmentation with average Dice 
scores of 84.22%, 92.48%, 88.70%, and 96.45%. These results not only significantly surpass most existing methods, but 
also demonstrate the powerful segmentation ability and excellent generalization performance of MS-DCSNet.

2  Related work
2.1  Medical image segmentation
Traditional U-shaped convolutional neural networks have 
been widely used in the field of medical image segmentation 
due to their advanced and effective design, which can be 
traced back to the introduction of U-Net [2]. The symmetric 
U-shaped structure consists of an encoder and a decoder, 
where the encoder is responsible for gradually extracting 
features and reducing spatial dimensions, while the decoder 
is responsible for gradually restoring spatial resolution 
and accurately locating the target segmentation area. Skip 
connections are used to preserve the original feature maps 
during the encoder’s downsampling process, combining 
them end-to-end with the decoder’s output to retain spatial 
detail information. This design concept of U-Net has laid 
an important foundation for the field of medical image seg­
mentation and has led to numerous subsequent improve­
ments. For example, ResU-Net [30] incorporates residual 
connections from ResNet [8] into the U-shaped structure,

2.2  Approaches for medical feature extraction
In recent years, multi-scale feature extraction technology 
has become a research hotspot in the field of computer 
vision for improving feature extraction capabilities. To 
improve the accuracy of image segmentation, researchers 
have proposed various innovative multi-scale feature fusion 
methods. For instance, CrossVit [45] introduces multi-scale 
features into the Vision Transformer, proposing a dual-
branch cross-attention mechanism that can simultaneously 
capture local details and global context information, signifi­
cantly enhancing the model’s ability to extract features from 
multi-scale targets. Atrous Spatial Pyramid Pooling (ASPP) 
[46] employs a multi-sampling rate dilated convolution 
strategy to effectively capture multi-scale contextual infor­
mation. Meanwhile, DeepLabv3+ [47] further enhances the 
model’s ability to fuse multi-scale features by improving the 
ASPP module and introducing a decoder structure. Feature 
Pyramid Network (FPN) [48] significantly enhances the 
model’s detection capability for multi-scale targets by con­
structing a bottom-up feature pyramid structure. Pyramid 
Medical Transformer [49] combines the pyramid structure 
with Transformers, significantly improving the accuracy of 
medical image segmentation through multi-scale feature 
encoding and decoding mechanisms. Additionally, transfer 
learning [50] has gradually become a widely used technol­
ogy in the field of medical imaging analysis. For example, 
Sheeba Armoogum et al. [51] proposed the method for cre­
ating classification models based on convolutional neural 
network deep learning and transfer learning, which achieved 
excellent accuracy on the breast cancer (BUSI) dataset [52], 
demonstrating the effectiveness of combining transfer learn­
ing with deep learning. HiFormer [53] proposed a hierar­
chical multi-scale feature fusion framework and applied 
the encoder to ImageNet [54] for pre-training, significantly 
improving segmentation performance. These methods have 
laid a solid foundation for subsequent research and become 
the main way to enhance feature extraction capabilities in 
the field of medical imaging analysis.
2.3  Research on channel shuffle
To explore more methods for enhancing feature extraction 
and representation, ShuffleNet [29] first proposed channel 
shuffle and applied it to mobile devices. The core mecha­
nism is to shuffle and reassemble the fixed arrangement of 
feature information in the channel according to certain rules 
before convolution operation, so that the model can learn 
more rich and diverse feature representations from differ­
ent sources within a limited receptive field. ShuffleNetV2 
[55] further optimizes the channel shuffle, proposing a more 
efficient feature fusion strategy that reduces memory access 
mitigating the problem of feature information loss through 
these connections and improving the network’s segmenta­
tion capability. ResECA-U-Net [31] innovatively integrates 
U-Net with ResNet34, effectively solving key challenges 
in radiation therapy for gastrointestinal cancer (GI) and 
achieving precise segmentation of organ regions. 3D U-Net 
[32] transforms traditional 2D image segmentation meth­
ods into 3D image segmentation through three-dimensional 
convolution operations, providing a new solution for image 
segmentation and significantly improving segmentation 
accuracy. However, these methods still face the issue of 
limited receptive fields and lack the ability to model global 
dependencies.
Since Dosovitskiy et al. [15] proposed the Vision Trans­
former and used self-attention mechanisms for long-range 
correlation modeling, the limitations of the receptive fields 
in convolutional neural networks have been improved and 
applied in the field of medical image segmentation [17]. Due 
to the quadratic complexity of the self-attention mechanism 
in Transformers, subsequent researchers have attempted to 
optimize this aspect, proposing numerous pure Transformer 
models or variants that combine Transformers with U-Net 
[23, 33–36], significantly reducing computational complex­
ity and enhancing overall model performance. Additionally, 
sequence modeling methods based on linear attention mech­
anisms, such as Mamba [37] and RWKV [38], have recently 
become a research focus and have been applied in computer 
vision and medical image segmentation [23, 24, 39]. For 
instance, U-Mamba [40] and SegMamba [41] integrate 
CNN and SSM modules, extracting local features through 
convolutional layers and combining them with long-range 
correlation modeling provided by SSM, demonstrating out­
standing performance in tasks such as organ segmentation, 
instrument segmentation, and cell segmentation. RWKV-
UNet [42] innovatively combines the RWKV (Receptance 
Weighted Key Value) structure with the U-Net structure, 
enhancing the model’s ability to capture long-range depen­
dencies and understand global context. These models not 
only achieve efficient linear complexity calculations but 
also capture long-range dependencies with high precision, 
fully extracting contextual information while maintaining 
low computational overhead, making them suitable for most 
computer vision tasks [43, 44]. Although models based on 
Transformer, Mamba, and RWKV architectures can effec­
tively model long-range correlations, the Transformer 
architecture often relies on multiple stacked computations 
to extract global features, while Mamba and RWKV archi­
tectures are still in an exploratory stage. Therefore, in this 
paper, we use a fully convolutional network architecture to 
explore the dynamic guided interaction between global and 
local features in medical image segmentation tasks, achiev­
ing efficient segmentation in complex environments.

3  Method
3.1  Overall architecture
The overall architecture of the proposed method is shown in 
Fig. 2. MS-DCSNet is based on U-shaped structure, consist­
ing of an encoder, a decoder, and skip connections, with a 
four-level structure. Specifically, the ConvStem (Fig. 2(f)) 
is responsible for changing the dimensions of the input fea­
ture map, while the encoder (Fig. 2(b)) and the decoder (Fig. 
2(c)) are responsible for adjusting the spatial resolution of 
the input feature map. Following the process by ConvStem, 
the dimensions of the feature maps obtained by each level of 
the encoder and decoder in the four-level structure are [48, 
96, 192, 384], and the channel shuffle strategy is set to [1, 2, 
4, 8] by default. This allows the feature information in the 
channels to be dynamically adjusted with the change of spa­
tial resolution, and achieves dynamic interaction between 
channel information to the maximum extent during dimen­
sion transformation and feature extraction.

3.3  MS-DCSA
The calculation process of MS-DCSA is shown in Fig. 3. 
Specifically, MS-DCSA has two branches. The first branch 
draws on the idea of the multi-head self-attention mecha­
nism from the Transformer, performing a linear project 
on the input features and using the first channel shuffle to 
disrupt the original feature order, resulting in multiple seg­
mented matrices. Subsequently, in order to fully utilize the 
rearranged feature information, we used convolution kernel 
sizes of 3 × 3, 5 × 5 and 7 × 7 to extract and concat the 
rearranged channel features at multiple scales, and then used 
channel shuffle again to rearrange the feature order, provid­
ing richer feature representations for subsequent network 
learning. Then, a linear project is used to convert the dimen­

4  Experiments
4.1  Experimental setup
4.1.1  Datasets
We conducted experiments on four different datasets: Syn­
apse [58], ACDC [59], ISIC2017 [60], and CVC-ClinicDB 
[61]. The Synapse dataset contains 3,779 axial abdominal 
clinical CT images from 30 abdominal CT scans, with each 
image containing 8 organs: Aorta, Gallbladder (GB), Left 
Kidney (KL), Right Kidney (KR), Liver, Pancreas (PC), 
Spleen (SP), and Stomach (SM). Based on previous work 
[17, 33], this dataset will be divided into training and test­
ing sets in a 6:4 ratio, and accuracy will be evaluated using 
the average Dice Similarity Coefficient (DSC) and the aver­
age 95% Hausdorff Distance (HD95). The ACDC dataset 
consists of 100 MRI images of heart scans, each contain­
ing three organs: Right Ventricle (RV), Myocardium (Myo), 
and Left Ventricle (LV). Following previous work [17, 33], 
we divide this dataset into training, validation, and testing 
sets in a 7:1:2 ratio, using DSC for performance evalua­
tion. ISIC2017 is a large-scale dermoscopic image classi­
fication dataset released by the International Skin Imaging 
Collaboration, containing 2,750 images, with 2,000 in the 
training set, 150 in the validation set, and 600 in the test­
ing set. In our experiments, we used 2,000 images from 
the training set, with 1,250 for training, 150 for validation, 
and 600 for testing. We evaluated prediction accuracy in 
this dataset using DSC, Sensitivity (Sen), Specificity (Spe), 
and Accuracy (Acc). CVC-ClinicDB is a biomedical data­
set extracted from 31 colonoscopy videos collected by the 
Barcelona Hospital Clinic, containing 612 images with a 
resolution of 384× 288. We divided the dataset into train­
ing, validation, and testing sets in a 7:1:2 ratio, using DSC, 
Intersection over Union (IoU), Precision (Pre), and Recall 
(Rec) to evaluate segmentation performance.

proposed MS-DCSA, which enables it to more effectively

mation exchange between groups, while choosing a larger 
grouping strategy can reduce the frequency of information 
exchange between groups, thereby reducing the computa­

model to learn richer feature representations, we selected [1, 
2, 4, 8] as the default grouping strategy.

5  Conclusion
In this paper, we propose MS-DCSNet, a novel fully con­
volutional network architecture that introduces MS-DCSA 
mechanism with inherent interpretability advantages for 
medical image segmentation. Unlike previous works that 
treat channel shuffle as a mere component to boost per­
formance, we systematically explore its role in building 
more robust and explainable feature representations. For 
example, our proposed MS-DCSA adopts an interleaved 
design of channel shuffle and multi-scale feature extraction 
and concatenation, which can disrupt the regular distribu­
tion of the original channels and enable the model to learn 
richer features at different scales. Furthermore, this design 
can also enable the model to break free from dependence 
on a single local channel feature and instead use global 
contextual information to correct local ambiguity. Also, it 
is worth noting that MS-DCSNet achieved significant seg­
mentation accuracy on multiple datasets, indicating its value 
in clinically challenging segmentation tasks. However, 
although MS-DCSNet performs well in 2D medical image 
segmentation tasks, its performance in 3D medical image 
segmentation tasks has not been validated. In practical clini­
cal application scenarios, it is usually more inclined to use 
3D volume data that contains complete spatial informa­
tion. Therefore, developing a 3D extended version of MS-
DCSNet and verifying its performance on more modalities
